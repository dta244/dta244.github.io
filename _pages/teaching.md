## Teaching Philosophy

I designed and taught a three-session "Introduction to Stata" workshop for researchers at the Vietnam Institute of Economics. The moment I knew it was working came near the end, when a participant sat there debugging her own do-file out loud, trying one fix at a time instead of turning to me to fix it for her. I teach for that, for students who can ask sharper questions, reason with evidence, and act on it without waiting for permission. In applied economics that means fluency in models, causal thinking, and data, enough that theory actually touches the messy reality it is supposed to explain. I approach a classroom the way I approach a research question. I form a hypothesis about what will help students learn, look for quick evidence (a poll, a problem set, a puzzled face in the third row), and revise before the next session. What I want from students is real conceptual understanding, quantitative and communication skills that hold up under questioning, a clear sense of how theory connects to policy and practice, and a room where high standards and honesty do not come at the cost of who feels welcome to speak.
